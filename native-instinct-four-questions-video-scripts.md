# Native Instinct: Four Questions Video Scripts

Built with the YouTube Shorts skill. Each block below is a spoken transcript only (about 60 seconds, 130 to 160 words), ready to paste into HeyGen or read to camera as Pinky Floyd. Everything inside a block is read aloud, so there are no labels, cues or stage directions inside.

Topic source: the "Where we start" section of the Native Instinct page.

The one thing each Short teaches:
1. A leak in a business is priced as frequency x time x hourly value, which makes small repeated tasks add up.
2. A three-day absence is a test, and "everything stops" means the work lives in your head and not in a system.
3. Knowing where revenue comes from and having it arrive on schedule are two separate problems, and the second one gets fixed at the handoffs.
4. A thirty-day plan needs only a priority, a first process and an order of steps.

The dollar example in Short 1 is simple arithmetic on made-up inputs, so verify the numbers if you change them.

---

```
Short 1

Here is how a twenty-minute task quietly costs you almost nine thousand dollars a year. Say you spend twenty minutes writing up client intake notes. Five times a week. That is a hundred minutes, close to two hours every week. Over fifty-two weeks it comes to about eighty-seven hours. Now value your own time at a hundred dollars an hour. That one small task costs you roughly eighty-seven hundred dollars a year, and it never once showed up as a line on a report. This is how businesses bleed. Not in one big hole, but in small repeated tasks nobody has priced. So we do the pricing first. Every task you repeat, how often you do it, how long it takes, and what that time is worth. Write it all down and the leaks show up fast. You cannot fix what you cannot see, and most owners have never seen it in dollars.
```

```
Short 2

There is a simple test for whether you own a business or a job. Imagine you disappear for three days. No phone, no email. Now watch what stops. Invoices waiting for your approval. A team member who cannot move because only you know the answer. A customer message sitting unanswered because the steps live in your head. If the honest answer is that everything stops, you do not have systems. You have one person doing six jobs, and that person is you. That is not a failure. It is what happens when you build something from nothing. The fix is surprisingly plain. Take the three tasks that eat the most of your time and write each one down. The name of the task, the tools it uses, who does what, the steps in order, and the point where quality gets checked. Once it is on paper, someone else can run it. Then three days away is just three days.
```

```
Short 3

Knowing where your revenue comes from and having it arrive on time are two different problems. Most owners solve the first one and assume that covers the second. You can name your sources. Referrals, repeat clients, the website, the phone. But if some months are full and others go quiet and you cannot say why, the sources are not the problem. The gaps between the steps are. A lead comes in and nobody follows up. A client finishes and nobody asks for a referral. Each of those handoffs depends on someone remembering, and remembering is not a system. So the work has two parts. First, map every source from the first contact all the way to a booked job, step by step. Then protect each handoff so it happens without you chasing it. Revenue that arrives steadily is not luck. It is a path somebody mapped and then guarded.
```

```
Short 4

You do not need a five-year plan to close a revenue gap. You need a thirty-day one, and it only has three parts. Start with your goal and where you are today. The space between them is the gap. Now ask what has to change first. The first part is the priority, which is the single place where time or money is leaking the most. Pick one, not five. The second part is the first process, the one task you write down so it stops living in your head and can be handed off. The third part is the order of the next thirty days, what happens first, second and third, starting right away. That is a plan you can actually begin on Monday. Big plans stall because they are too big to start. A small first move, aimed at the biggest leak, is how a gap actually starts to close.
```
