# Native Instinct: Four Questions Video Scripts (Pinky's voice)

Rewritten in Pinky's voice: a woman at her own kitchen table, talking to one person she likes. Each block is a spoken transcript only (about 60 seconds), so everything inside is read aloud. There are no labels or stage directions inside the blocks.

Topic source: the "Where we start" section of the Native Instinct page.

Each Short has a laugh, one concrete detail, and one coined line a viewer can carry out:
1. "A leak with good manners."
2. "A job wearing a business card."
3. "Remembering is not a system. It is a prayer."
4. "A big plan in a binder just sits on a shelf looking smart."

Note: the dollar figures in Short 1 are illustrative arithmetic on made-up inputs. No personal anecdotes or facts about Pinky were invented. If she wants a real story in any Short, add one in place of the example.

---

```
Short 1

Okay, so let me tell you how a twenty-minute task is quietly robbing you of almost nine thousand dollars a year. Say you write up client intake notes. Twenty minutes, five times a week. Honey, that is nearly two hours every single week. Fifty-two weeks of that is about eighty-seven hours. Now put a hundred dollars an hour on your time, because you are not free, baby. That comes to roughly eighty-seven hundred dollars, and it never showed up on one report. That is what I call a leak with good manners. It does not flood the kitchen. It just drips while you walk past it with your coffee. So we price it first. What you repeat, how often, how long, and what it costs. Do you understand what I am saying? You cannot fix a leak you have never seen, and most owners have never once seen theirs in dollars.
```

```
Short 2

Now listen. Here is a little test. Pretend you are gone for three days. Phone off, no email, sitting somewhere with a sweet tea. What stops? The invoices waiting on your okay. Your team standing around because only you know the answer. A customer message sitting there because the steps live in your head. If the honest answer is everything, sit with that a minute. You do not have systems. You have one person doing six jobs, and that person is you. Now, I am not fussing at you. You built that from nothing, and that is how it gets built. But a business that cannot breathe without you is a job wearing a business card. So here is the fix, and it is plain. Take the three tasks that eat your day and write them down. The tools, who does what, the steps in order, and where quality gets checked. Then three days away is just three days.
```

```
Short 3

Okay, so you can tell me exactly where your revenue comes from. Referrals, repeat clients, the website, the phone. Good. Now here is the thing. Knowing where it comes from is not the same as it showing up on time. Honey, I know owners who can name every source and still have a feast month followed by a funeral. And when that happens, the sources are rarely the problem. The gaps are. A lead comes in and nobody follows up. A client finishes and nobody asks for a referral. Every one of those handoffs hangs on somebody remembering, and remembering is not a system. It is a prayer. So the work has two parts. Map every source from first hello to booked job. Then guard each handoff so it happens without you chasing it. Steady revenue is not luck. It is a path somebody mapped and then watched.
```

```
Short 4

Baby, put the five-year plan down. You do not need it. You need thirty days, and it has three parts. First, know your goal and where you stand today. The space between them is the gap. Now, what has to change first? Part one is the priority, the single place where time or money leaks the most. One. Not five. Part two is the first process, the one task you write down so it stops living in your head. Part three is the order of the next thirty days, what happens first, second and third, starting now, not after the holidays. A big plan in a binder just sits on a shelf looking smart. Big plans stall because they are too big to start. A small first move, aimed at the biggest leak, is how a gap actually closes. Start there.
```
